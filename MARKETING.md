# BIDTOBE1 — marketing playbook

Two jobs, and they are different. **(A)** market BIDTOBE1 itself. **(B)** deliver results for the business sitting at #1 — because their screenshot is your entire ad budget.

---

# PART A — Marketing BIDTOBE1

## The one strategic truth

A paid leaderboard has a **two-sided cold start**: bidders won't pay without spectators, spectators won't come without bids. Outbid solved it with a viral launch moment and had ~10 clones within 24 hours. You cannot out-viral them from New Zealand. **You win on depth in one vertical, not spectacle.**

So: **do not launch BIDTOBE1 nationally. Launch one board.**

> **#1 AI & Automation Agency — Auckland**

One city. One category. Make that single crown feel worth owning, then repeat the playbook per category.

## Phase 0 — Seed before you sell (week 1–2, $0)

Empty boards kill the product. Before anyone can bid:

1. Scrape/compile **40–60 real Auckland AI, automation and web agencies** (Google Maps, LinkedIn, NZTech member list, Startup Auckland).
2. **List them all for free**, unranked, with a real profile — logo, pitch, services, AI Visibility Score.
3. Email each one: *"You're listed at #14 on the Auckland AI board with a visibility score of 71. Here's your dashboard link — it's free, no card. If you want #1 it's currently $85."*

That email is the entire go-to-market. It converts because the loss is already visible — you're not selling a listing, you're showing them a rank they don't like.

**Target: 8 paying listings by day 14.** That's proof, not profit.

## Phase 1 — Manufacture the first bidding war

A board with one bidder is a directory. A board with two rivals is a sport.

- Identify **two agencies that genuinely compete** and email both the same day.
- The moment one is outbid, the alert fires: *"Southern Automation just took #1 in Auckland AI. You're #2. $30 puts you back on top."*
- **Outbid alerts are your highest-ROI feature.** Free to send, directly cause the next payment. Build them before anything else.

## Phase 2 — Content that compounds (the real moat)

Outbid gets traffic from X. **You should get traffic from search and AI answers**, which doesn't decay when the meme dies.

Programmatic pages, generated from data you already hold:

| Page type | Example | Why it ranks |
|---|---|---|
| Category × city | "Best AI Automation Companies in Auckland (2026)" | The exact query buyers type |
| Compare | "Kushal AI vs Southern Automation" | High-intent, near-zero competition |
| Cost guides | "What does an AI receptionist cost in NZ?" | Top-of-funnel, links to the board |
| Rank movements | "Who's #1 in Auckland AI this month" | Freshness signal, monthly repeat |

With 10 categories × 8 cities × 4 page types ≈ **320 indexable pages** from one dataset.

**AI-citation play (do not skip this):** buyers increasingly ask ChatGPT/Gemini/Perplexity for "best AI agency in Auckland". Publish `/api/board` as clean structured data, add `ItemList` + `LocalBusiness` JSON-LD to every page, keep an `llms.txt`. Being *the* machine-readable NZ business dataset is worth more in three years than the leaderboard is today.

## Phase 3 — Distribution channels, ranked by ROI for NZ

1. **Direct outbound email** — 60 businesses, personalised with their actual rank. Highest conversion by far.
2. **LinkedIn, not X.** NZ business owners live on LinkedIn; X is US indie hackers who will never buy from an Auckland plumber's board. Post every rank change: *"Someone just paid $340 to be #1 AI agency in Auckland. Here's what that bought them."*
3. **Local trade/industry Facebook groups** — NZ Tradies, Auckland Business Network. Value first, never a raw link drop.
4. **The #1 business's own bragging** — give them a **"#1 in Auckland" embeddable badge** for their website. Free backlinks + social proof, and it makes losing the spot hurt more.
5. **NZ tech media** — NZ Herald Business, BusinessDesk, The Spinoff Business, Idealog. Angle: *"NZ businesses are now bidding against each other to be #1 — a Kiwi take on the auction that made $255k in a week."* Local + slightly controversial = coverage.
6. **Paid ads: don't, yet.** Your CAC has to be under one listing ($10–100). Outbound email is free.

## Phase 4 — Retention (where the money actually is)

A one-off $50 bid is a bad business. These make it recurring:

- **Outbid alerts** → email + SMS the moment someone passes them.
- **Auto-defend** → "hold my #1 up to $500/mo", card on file. Converts bidding wars into subscription revenue. **Highest-leverage unbuilt feature.**
- **Monthly rank report** → "You were #2 for 19 days, got 340 clicks, 12 enquiries, cost per enquiry $7.10." Attaches your product to their revenue.
- **Momentum board decay** → already built. Old spend expires, so staying #1 requires paying again. This is the mechanic that makes BIDTOBE1 a subscription instead of a one-off.

## Launch-week checklist

- [ ] 50 Auckland AI/automation businesses seeded free with real profiles
- [ ] Outbid alert emails live
- [ ] Embeddable "#1 in Auckland" badge
- [ ] JSON-LD + `llms.txt` on every board and profile page
- [ ] Personalised outbound email to all 50 (their rank in the subject line)
- [ ] LinkedIn post the first bidding war, with the screenshot
- [ ] Pitch one NZ business journalist
- [ ] Privacy policy + paid-placement disclosure live (legal requirement, see below)

## Metrics that matter

| Metric | Target month 1 | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Paying listings | 8 | Proof the mechanic works locally |
| Repeat bid rate | >40% | The only real health metric |
| Avg raises per listing | >2 | Means competition, not just listing |
| Clicks per listing | >100 | What you're actually selling |
| Enquiries delivered | >20 | What makes them renew |
| Cost per enquiry shown | <$15 | Must beat Google Ads or they churn |

**Vanity metrics to ignore:** total visitors, total bid volume, listing count.

## Legal guardrails (NZ-specific, non-negotiable)

- **Fair Trading Act** — paid placement must be obvious on every screen. Already in the ticker, hero, rules and footer. Never imply rank = quality.
- **Testimonials must be evidenced.** The `/about` quotes are labelled placeholders — replace only with things you can prove.
- **Privacy Act 2020** — leads are personal information. Privacy policy, retention period, deletion on request.
- **Unsolicited Electronic Messages Act** — cold *email* to a business address is generally fine; make sure every message has a working unsubscribe and a physical address.

# PART B — Marketing the business that wins #1

Their result is your marketing. If #1 doesn't work, the board dies. So productise this — hand it to every winner automatically.

## The "you won #1" onboarding pack

The moment someone takes #1, auto-email them:

**1. Fix the pitch first.** Rank buys the impression; the one-liner buys the click. Rewrite it as outcome + specificity + proof:
- ❌ "AI automation solutions for business"
- ✅ "AI receptionist that answers every after-hours call — used by 40 Auckland clinics"

**2. Match the landing page to the board.** Their listing promised one thing; the homepage must repeat it above the fold. Biggest single conversion leak.

**3. Take the screenshot.** Give them a pre-rendered "🥇 #1 AI Agency in Auckland — BIDTOBE1" image, sized for LinkedIn, plus draft copy. Their post markets them *and* you.

**4. Put the badge on their site.** Embeddable, links back. Backlink for you, trust signal for them.

**5. Tell them their real numbers.** Cost per click and cost per enquiry are already on the dashboard. Teach them the break-even maths:

> LTV $2,000 × 30% close rate = $600 per qualified enquiry.
> At $340 spend and 12 enquiries → $28 each. Break-even at ~1 in 21.

That calculation is why someone pays $17,000 elsewhere, and it's what turns a nervous $50 bidder into a $500 one.

**6. Warn them about the drop.** "You'll be outbid eventually. Turn on auto-defend, or plan a re-take." Sets expectations and sells the retention feature in the same breath.

## What to tell them honestly

- A click is not a customer. Judge it on enquiries, not clicks.
- Week one always outperforms — novelty traffic is real but fades.
- If their cost per enquiry beats Google Ads, stay. If not, leave. Saying this openly is why they'll trust the board.
